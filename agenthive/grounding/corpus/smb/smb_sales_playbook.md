# Sales playbook — discovery, qualification and follow-up

Owner: Lena Okafor, VP Sales. Last reviewed 2026-10-15. Synthetic demo document — Kitewing Software is fictional.

## Who we sell to

**Ideal customer:** a service contractor with 10-300 field technicians, recurring maintenance work and a painful scheduling process (whiteboards, spreadsheets or a legacy on-premise tool). Best-fit segments: HVAC and plumbing, facilities management, fire and security, elevator maintenance, pest control and landscaping.

**Personas**

| Persona | Typical title | Cares about | Role in the deal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Operations leader | COO, Operations Director | Overtime, first-time fix, growth without more office staff | Often the economic buyer |
| Dispatch lead | Dispatch & Operations Manager | Saturday shifts, re-keying jobs, technicians calling the office | Usually the champion |
| Finance | CFO, Controller | Cash flow (DSO), price per technician, payback | Approves above the COO's limit |
| IT | IT Manager | Security, SSO, data migration, integrations | Technical evaluator, can block |
| Field | Branch managers, lead technicians | A mobile app that works offline and is simple | Users; demo audience |

## Pipeline stages and exit criteria

| Stage | Probability | Exit criteria |
|---|---|---|
| Prospecting | 5% | Meeting booked with someone who feels the pain |
| Discovery | 10% | Pain, metrics and a compelling event confirmed; economic buyer identified |
| Solution Validation | 30% | Tailored demo done; decision criteria and process confirmed; champion tested |
| Proposal | 50% | Proposal and ROI reviewed with the economic buyer |
| Negotiation | 75% | Verbal yes; legal and security review under way |
| Closed Won / Lost | 100% / 0% | Order form signed / loss reason recorded |

Never move a deal to Proposal without meeting the economic buyer.

## Qualification: MEDDICC

Use **MEDDICC** for deals above $25,000 ACV and **BANT** for Starter deals and SDR qualification. Score each criterion 0-3 (0 unknown, 1 weak or assumed, 2 partially confirmed, 3 confirmed by the buyer in their own words). Weights: Metrics 15, Economic buyer 15, Decision criteria 10, Decision process (including the paper process — legal, security, procurement) 15, Identify pain 20, Champion 15, Competition 10.

- **75% or more:** qualified — advance to Solution Validation.
- **50-74%:** qualified with gaps — stay in Discovery and close the gaps before the demo.
- **Below 50%:** not yet qualified — more discovery or nurture.

Red flags that stop a deal moving forward: no economic buyer identified, pain not quantified, no tested champion, or no dated decision process.

## The 30-minute discovery call

1. **0-3 min** — introductions, purpose, agenda and time check.
2. **3-15 min** — current process and the "worst dispatch day" story; what they have tried.
3. **15-22 min** — impact: baseline numbers (overtime %, first-time fix, drive hours, invoice lag, DSO) and the cost of doing nothing.
4. **22-27 min** — decision process, people, timeline and the compelling event (end of support, peak season, renewal of a contract).
5. **27-30 min** — agree one **dated** next step: tailored demo, ROI workshop or a meeting with the economic buyer.

Good discovery questions are specific to the prospect: use their system names, their branches and their dates. Ask pain and metrics questions before anything about budget.

## Follow-up rules

- Send the recap email **within 24 hours** of the call.
- The recap uses the buyer's own words for the pain, repeats the numbers they gave, summarises what we understood about the decision process and proposes **one dated next step**.
- Do not attach pricing to a first recap; give a ballpark range only if they asked.
- Attach at most one case study, matched to their segment, and only one we are allowed to name (see case studies).
- Log the call in the CRM the same day: stage, MEDDICC score, next step and close date.
