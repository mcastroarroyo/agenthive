# Quillmark Private Wealth — Investment Policy & Suitability Standard (QPW-POL-07)

*Synthetic policy for the Wursta Agent Hive demo. Quillmark Bank and Quillmark Private Wealth are fictional. Rules
and thresholds are illustrative, not regulatory guidance.*

**Owner:** Head of Private Wealth Supervision · **Version:** 7.2 · **Effective:** 2026-01-01

## 1. Purpose and scope

This standard governs every recommendation an advisor makes to a Quillmark Private Wealth client: portfolio
construction, rebalancing, single-product purchases and responses to client-requested trades. A recommendation may
only be presented when it is suitable for the client's documented profile, consistent with the client's mandate
and recorded in a record of advice (§8).

## 2. Model portfolios and tolerance bands

Each client is mapped to a model portfolio by risk score (1-10). Asset-class targets and bands:

| Model | Risk score | Equity | Fixed income | Alternatives | Cash |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Conservative Income | 1-3 | 30% ±5 | 60% ±5 | 5% ±3 | 5% ±3 |
| Balanced | 4-6 | 55% ±5 | 35% ±5 | 7% ±3 | 3% ±2 |
| Growth | 7-8 | 75% ±5 | 17% ±5 | 6% ±3 | 2% ±2 |
| Aggressive Growth | 9-10 | 88% ±5 | 5% ±3 | 5% ±3 | 2% ±2 |

**Liquidity override.** When the client's documented 12-month liquidity need is larger than the model cash target,
the cash target is raised to cover it and the other targets are scaled down proportionally.

## 3. Suitability rules (WS rules)

- **WS-1 Profile currency.** The suitability profile must have been reviewed within the last 12 months and the KYC
  refresh must not be overdue. If either fails, no new recommendation may be presented: the advisor must first
  complete a profile review with the client.
- **WS-2 Client restrictions.** Holdings or purchases that conflict with a documented exclusion (e.g. a
  fossil-fuel-free trust deed) must be exited/blocked. Exclusions apply to direct holdings and sector funds, not to
  diversified index funds.
- **WS-3 Complex products.** Leveraged/inverse products, private-credit interval funds and digital-asset products
  are complex. They require risk score 6 or higher **and** a documented complex-product eligibility flag. Complex
  products in total may not exceed 10% of the portfolio; leveraged products may not exceed 5%.
- **WS-4 Digital assets.** Maximum 5% of the portfolio and only for risk score 7 or higher.
- **WS-5 Single-issuer concentration.** No single issuer (individual stock or bond issuer) above 10% of the
  portfolio. Concentrated low-basis positions may be reduced in stages (see §5).
- **WS-6 Sector concentration.** Single-sector funds are capped at 20%. Look-through exposure to one sector above
  30% of the portfolio is a soft limit requiring advisor review and a documented rationale.
- **WS-7 Asset-class drift.** Any asset class outside its band triggers a rebalance back to target.
- **WS-8 Liquidity.** Cash and money-market holdings must cover the documented 12-month liquidity need.
- **WS-9 Illiquid holdings.** Illiquid products (interval funds) are capped at 15%; redemptions only at tender
  windows.
- **WS-10 High yield.** High-yield bonds are capped at 10% for Conservative clients and 15% otherwise.
- **WS-11 Order validity.** A sale may not exceed the position held.
- **WS-12 Insider / employer stock.** Trades in a client's employer stock need compliance pre-clearance and must
  fall inside the issuer's open trading window.

## 4. Client-requested trades

When a client asks for a trade that fails a WS rule, the advisor must explain which rule and why, and may offer a
compliant alternative (for example the maximum permitted size). Unsolicited orders that would breach WS-2, WS-3 or
WS-4 are not accepted without Supervision approval and are flagged for review.

## 5. Rebalancing and tax awareness

- Cure policy breaches first (WS-2, WS-3, WS-4, WS-5, WS-6, WS-10), then return asset classes to target.
- Sell satellite positions (sector, regional or single-stock holdings) before core model funds; buy the model's
  core funds.
- In taxable accounts, sell loss lots first, then long-term lots with the highest cost basis; avoid realising
  short-term gains unless the holding breaches policy. Tax-deferred accounts ignore tax lots.
- Concentrated single-issuer positions with an embedded gain above 50% of market value may be reduced in four
  quarterly tranches, re-confirming price, trading window and tax position before each tranche.
- Minimum trade size USD 2,500. Estimated trading cost: 3 bps for funds, 5 bps for single stocks.
- Tax figures shown to clients are illustrative and must say so; clients are referred to their tax adviser.

## 6. Client communication standard

A client proposal is written in plain language: why we suggest a change, what changes, what it costs (with the
illustrative tax estimate), what stays the same, and the market themes that matter. No rule codes, no jargon, no
performance promises or price targets. Market commentary must cite its source and date.

## 7. Supervision

Supervisors review every proposal before it is sent. Proposals with any WS-1 to WS-5 exception, or tax impact
above USD 50,000, require a second review by the Branch Supervisory Principal.

## 8. Record of advice

Every recommendation is filed as a record of advice within T+1 and approved by the supervisor. The record contains
the rationale, the trades, the suitability verdict and the client's approval. Records are retained for 7 years.
No order is placed before the client approves.
