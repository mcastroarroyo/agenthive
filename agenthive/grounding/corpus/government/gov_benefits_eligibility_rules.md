# Household Assistance Programmes — Eligibility Rules 2026

Port Alder Department of Human Services (DHS), 75 Bayview Avenue, (555) 010-0420. Fictional document for
demonstration purposes. Applies to applications received from 1 January 2026.

## 1. How income is counted

Eligibility uses **gross monthly household income** — all earnings, pensions, benefits, child support and
self-employment profit before tax, for everyone who lives in the home and shares meals. Income limits are a percentage
of the 2026 Area Median Income (AMI) for Port Alder, which is $88,000 a year for a four-person household.

**Monthly gross income limits (USD)**

| 2 | 2,933 | 3,520 | 4,693 | 4,987 |
| 3 | 3,300 | 3,960 | 5,280 | 5,610 |
| 4 | 3,667 | 4,400 | 5,867 | 6,233 |

For households larger than eight, add 8% of the four-person limit for each additional person.

## 2. Programmes

**Alder Food Assist (AFA)** — monthly food benefit on the Alder Assist card. Income at or below 50% AMI. Estimated
benefit = maximum allotment − 12% × (gross monthly income − $500 standard deduction), never less than $25. Maximum
allotments: 1 person $210, 2 $385, 3 $550, 4 $700, 5 $830, 6 $995, 7 $1,100, 8 $1,255 (+$155 per extra person).
Decision within 30 days; **expedited service within 7 days** when monthly income is under $150 and cash on hand is
under $100, or when rent and utilities exceed income. Certification lasts 12 months.

**Utility Relief Credit (URC)** — 30% credit on the water, sewer and trash bill, capped at $45 a month. Income at or
below 60% AMI. The applicant must be the account holder, or show that utilities are included in the rent. Decision
within 20 days; renew every 12 months.

**Rental Bridge Assistance (RBA)** — one-time payment of up to three months of rent, maximum $4,500, paid directly to
the landlord to prevent an eviction. Requirements: renter household; income at or below 80% AMI; an eviction notice
or at least one month of rent arrears; at least six months living in Port Alder. Available once every 24 months.
Decision within 10 days; payment within 5 business days of approval. The landlord must complete the landlord payment
form (tax ID and bank details) — DHS never pays the tenant directly.

**Child Care Subsidy (CCS)** — pays licensed child care for children under 13 (under 18 with a disability) while the
parent or parents work, study or train at least 20 hours a week. Income at or below 85% AMI. The family pays a
co-payment of 7% of gross income when income is at or below 60% AMI, otherwise 10%. Care must be with a licensed
provider. Decision within 30 days.

**Senior & Disability Transit Pass (SDTP)** — monthly transit pass for $5 instead of $68 for residents aged 65 or older
or with a certified disability. **No income test.** The pass is free for households enrolled in Alder Food Assist.
Decision within 10 days; the card is mailed and activated at any ticket machine or in the transit app.

## 3. Documents

**Every application:** photo ID for the applicant (national ID card or passport); proof of Port Alder address dated
within the last 60 days (lease, utility bill or official letter); national ID numbers for all household members
(birth certificates for children without an ID); proof of all household income for the last 30 days.

**Programme-specific:** AFA — most recent bank statement. URC — latest water/sewer bill or a lease showing utilities
included. RBA — signed lease, eviction notice or landlord's arrears statement, landlord payment form, proof of six
months' residence. CCS — employer letter or work schedule (or school enrolment) showing 20+ hours a week, birth
certificate for each child in care, licensed provider's enrolment form. SDTP — proof of age, or disability
certification (form DHS-DC1 signed by a licensed clinician, or the national disability card).

One set of common documents serves every programme applied for at the same time.

## 4. Applying, missing documents and decisions

Apply on the DHS portal, by photo upload on the messaging channel, or in person at 75 Bayview Avenue. When documents
are missing, DHS sends a notice with a due date at least 10 days away. **If the documents are not received by the due
date, the application is denied for missing information**; the household may reapply at any time. Screening tools,
including the Benefits agent, give a preliminary view only — DHS caseworkers make the final determination.

## 5. Appeals

A household may appeal any decision in writing within **30 days of the date on the notice**. Appeals are heard by a
DHS hearing officer who was not involved in the decision; benefits continue during an appeal of a reduction. A change
in circumstances (for example reduced working hours) can be reported at any time as a new application.
