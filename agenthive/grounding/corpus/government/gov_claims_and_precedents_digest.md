# Claims Procedure and Precedent Digest — Office of the City Attorney

Litigation & Claims Section, City of Port Alder. Fictional document for demonstration purposes: the statute,
decisions and courts below are invented for training and demo use and are not real law.

## 1. Government claims procedure (Valdoria Government Claims Act, as applied by PAMC 2.60)

- **Filing:** a claim for money damages against the City must be filed with the City Clerk within **180 days** of the
  incident. Late claims may be accepted only on an application for leave showing excusable neglect, filed within one
  year.
- **City decision:** the City must accept or reject the claim within **45 days** of filing; if it does not act, the
  claim is deemed rejected on day 45.
- **Lawsuit:** after a **written rejection notice**, the claimant may sue within **6 months** of the date of the notice
  (2 years if no written notice was sent).
- **Fees:** attorney fees are **not recoverable** against the City in a property-damage claim unless a statute
  provides for them.
- **Settlement authority (PAMC 2.60.090):** up to $5,000 the Risk Manager; up to $25,000 the City Attorney; above
  $25,000 the City Council in closed session.

## 2. Liability for street defects

The City has a **ministerial duty** to repair known dangerous defects in public streets within a reasonable time. The
decision of *which* streets to resurface and when is **discretionary** and immune. Liability for a defect requires:
(1) a dangerous condition; (2) **actual or constructive notice** in time to repair; (3) failure to repair or warn
within a reasonable time; (4) causation and damages.

## 3. Precedents

**Marlowe v. City of Port Alder (Alder County Superior Court, 2021).** A 311 report describing a pothole gave the City
**actual notice**. The City repaired it 12 days later, outside its published 72-hour target; a cyclist was injured on
day 6. Held: the City breached its ministerial duty; the published service standard was strong evidence of a
reasonable repair time. Award reduced 20% for the cyclist's comparative fault (riding at night without a light).

**Ventura Haulage Ltd. v. City of Port Alder (Valdoria Court of Appeal, 2019).** Published service-level targets are
**evidence of a reasonable time, not a guarantee**. The City may rebut with **contemporaneous records** of resource
constraints (crew logs, material shortages, competing emergencies). Undocumented constraints raised after the fact
carry little weight. Judgment for the City where crew diversion to a water-main break was logged in real time.

**Pierce v. Eastgate Transit Authority (Valdoria Court of Appeal, 2017).** Comparative fault applies to vehicle-damage
claims. Driving **10% or more above the posted limit** on a wet road supports a reduction of **25–40%**; driving at or
under the limit with lights on supports no reduction.

**Lindqvist v. City of Port Alder (Alder County Superior Court, 2022).** Depressions shallower than **1.5 inches
(3.8 cm)** are trivial defects as a matter of law; no liability. Depth must be proven with a photo or measurement close
to the incident date.

**Hollis Paving Co. v. Bayshore County (Valdoria Court of Appeal, 2020).** Bid-protest deadlines in a public
procurement code are **mandatory**. A protest filed after the deadline is barred even if it might have succeeded on the
merits; courts will not reach the merits on a writ petition when administrative remedies were not timely exhausted.

**Stratton Builders v. City of Port Alder (Alder County Superior Court, 2018).** A missing bid bond is a **material,
non-waivable defect**. Bid security delivered after bid opening cannot cure the defect, because it would let the
bidder decide after seeing competitors' prices whether to be bound.

**Reyes Electric v. Northpoint Water District (Valdoria Court of Appeal, 2016).** A local-preference rule must be
applied **exactly as published**, including any cap. Applying the preference within the cap is lawful; exceeding it
invalidates the award.

**Calder v. City of Port Alder (Valdoria Court of Appeal, 2023).** Where a public contract has been executed and work
has begun, courts weigh the public interest and prejudice to the contractor before setting aside an award; the
usual remedy for a successful but late-discovered protest is bid-preparation costs, not re-award.

## 4. Practice notes

1. **Preserve evidence early:** issue a litigation hold for 311 records, work orders, crew logs, materials logs,
   photos and emails as soon as a claim is filed. Missing contemporaneous records weaken the Ventura defence.
2. **Value claims realistically:** start from documented repair invoices; rental cars only for the reasonable repair
   period; no attorney fees; apply comparative fault from objective evidence (dashcam speed, weather, lighting).
3. **Small claims:** property-damage claims under $10,000 are usually cheaper to settle at documented damages less
   comparative fault than to litigate; consider the precedent effect for similar claims on the same street.
4. **Records requests by litigants:** coordinate public-records responses with the case team; records are not exempt
   because of the litigation, but privileged material is.
