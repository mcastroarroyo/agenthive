## 3. First Home Subsidy

A **$6,000 grant** for first-time buyers with **household income of $2,500 a month or less**, used for the down
payment and closing costs on an NHFF First Home Loan. The subsidy is paid to the seller's account at closing and is
not repayable if the household lives in the home for at least 5 years. One subsidy per household, ever.

## 4. Affordability rules

- **Housing ratio:** principal + interest + property tax + home insurance must not exceed **30% of gross monthly
  income for public products** (NHFF) and **33% for partner lenders and the Development Bank**.
- **Debt-to-income (DTI):** housing payment plus all other monthly debt payments must not exceed **40% (NHFF)**,
  **43% (partner lenders and the Development Bank)** or **45% (Bayline personal loans)**.
- **Adjustable-rate loans** are tested at the stress rate (7.85% for NGS-ARM51), not the starting rate.
- Planning assumptions: **property tax 0.8% of the price per year**, **home insurance $35 per month**, **closing costs
  about 3% of the price** (paid in cash at closing).
- The down payment must come from savings, a gift letter from family, or the First Home Subsidy — not from another loan.

## 5. Comparing offers (advisor practice)

Treat the household's needs as the requirements and each product as a proposal: check every requirement (down payment,
income cap, ratios, loan maximum, conditions such as membership or certified homes), discard products that fail, then
compare the remaining ones on **monthly payment, total interest over the term and upfront cash**. Prefer fixed rates for
households whose budget cannot absorb a payment rise; explain that a lower adjustable rate can rise after year 5.
For repairs under $8,000 on an owner-occupied home, the NHFF microloan is normally cheaper than a personal loan.

## 6. Pre-qualification documents

National ID for every borrower; proof of income for the last 3 months (pay slips, pension statements or
self-employment ledger and tax return); bank statements for the last 3 months showing the savings; list of current debts
with monthly payments; for purchases, the seller's price quote or promise of sale; for repairs, a contractor quote and
proof of ownership. Advisors and this guide provide information only — **approval is a lender's credit decision**.
