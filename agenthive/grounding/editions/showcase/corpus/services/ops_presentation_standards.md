# Presentation & Brand Standards (client decks)

**Owner:** Marketing & Brand team · **Last reviewed:** 2026-09-28 · **Status:** current
**Applies to:** every client-facing deck, workshop deck and executive briefing produced by Sablewood staff or agents
(including Scribe). Synthetic demo document.

## 1. Deck length and structure
- **Client decks:** 6-15 slides. **Executive decks:** at most 10 slides. Anything longer goes into an appendix or a
  leave-behind document.
- Required structure:
  1. **Title slide** — title, subtitle (client or audience), date. No bullet list.
  2. **Agenda** — within the first three slides.
  3. **Context / problem** — in the client's words.
  4. **Approach** — what we would do and why it fits.
  5. **Proof** — one or two case studies with measurable outcomes and a source.
  6. **Plan** — phases, timeline, team.
  7. **Next steps** — the last slide, with a clear call to action (a workshop, a pilot scope, a decision).

## 2. Writing rules
- **Action titles:** a title states the takeaway ("Triage time fell 42% in 12 weeks"), at most 10 words.
- **Bullets:** at most 5 per slide and at most 14 words per bullet. If you need more, split the slide.
- **Speaker notes:** required on every slide except the title slide; at least 15 words, ideally 40-120. Notes say what
  the presenter adds beyond the slide — context, the story, the question to ask the audience.
- **Figures need a source:** every percentage, multiplier or money figure carries a footnote with the case study id
  (e.g. "Source: CS-014") or the report it came from. Never round a figure up to make it sound better.
- **Plain language:** explain acronyms the first time; write for the audience (a CIO deck is not an engineering review).

## 3. What must never appear in a client deck
- Internal commercials: **margins**, **rate cards**, **discounts**, **cost to serve**, internal pricing floors.
- Internal-only notes: anything marked "internal", "internal only", "confidential" or "do not share", and any mention of
  exclusivity discussions.
- Unsupported claims: "guarantee", "100%", "world-class", "best-in-class", "#1", "risk-free". Describe measured results
  instead.
- Other clients' names unless they are approved named references (see section 4).

## 5. Templates and visual identity
- Templates: **sablewood_client_2026** (default), **sablewood_executive_2026** (board and C-level), **sablewood_workshop_2026**
  (interactive sessions with exercises).
- Use the template's colours and fonts; do not paste screenshots of other companies' logos.
- One idea per slide. Prefer a simple diagram or a single chart over a table of text.
- Architecture diagrams use the Google Cloud icon set and show data flow left to right.

## 6. Accessibility
- Minimum 18 pt body text; contrast ratio of at least 4.5:1.
- Every image and chart has alt text describing what it shows.
- Do not use colour as the only way to convey meaning (add labels or patterns).

## 7. Review and sharing
- Drafts are shared only inside Sablewood (company accounts). The engagement lead reviews before anything is sent.
- Decks built by agents (Scribe) run the automated brand check; a deck with any **error** cannot be created, and the
  target brand score is 80 or higher.
- Final decks are shared as view-only links; never attach editable source files to external email.
