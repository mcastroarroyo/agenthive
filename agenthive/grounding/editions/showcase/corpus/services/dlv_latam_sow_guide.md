# LATAM SOW Guide: Language, Currency, Tax and Local Clauses

**Owner:** Sablewood LATAM Delivery & Legal · **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-20 · **Status:** current
Synthetic demo document. Tax figures are illustrative planning values — always validate with local tax counsel and
Sablewood Finance before quoting.

## 1. When to use the LATAM SOW
Use the LATAM SOW for clients domiciled in Mexico, Colombia, Brazil, Chile, Peru or El Salvador, or whenever the
client asks for a Spanish or Portuguese contract. The structure is the same as the global SOW standard; headings
and approved clauses are in the contract language.

## 2. Contract language
- Spanish (es): Mexico, Colombia, Chile, Peru, El Salvador. Portuguese (pt): Brazil.
- Brazilian public bodies and most large private clients require the contract in Portuguese. When a bilingual
  version is signed, state which language prevails.
- Spanish headings: Resumen ejecutivo; Alcance; Entregables; Criterios de aceptación; Supuestos; Fuera de alcance;
  Responsabilidades del cliente; Hitos y cronograma; Precio y forma de pago; Control de cambios; Gobierno del
  proyecto; Consideraciones fiscales y legales; Firmas.
- Portuguese headings: Resumo executivo; Escopo; Entregáveis; Critérios de aceite; Premissas; Fora do escopo;
  Responsabilidades do cliente; Marcos e cronograma; Preço e forma de pagamento; Controle de mudanças; Governança;
  Considerações fiscais e legais; Assinaturas.

## 3. Currency and FX
- Price in USD and show the local-currency equivalent at the planning FX rate of the month (November 2026 planning
  rates: MXN 18.50, COP 4,050, BRL 5.40, CLP 940, PEN 3.75; El Salvador uses USD).
- If the price is fixed in local currency (local-partner billing), add an FX adjustment clause when the rate moves
  more than 5% between signature and invoice.

## 4. Billing scenarios
**A. Cross-border (Sablewood US entity invoices in USD).** The client may be required to withhold income tax on
payments abroad. Our fees are quoted net of withholding: the gross-up clause makes the client increase the payment
so Sablewood receives the agreed amount. Illustrative planning withholding rates: Mexico 25% (treaty relief to 0% may be
available with a US tax-residency certificate), Colombia 20% (no US income tax treaty), Brazil 15% IRRF plus CIDE
and import taxes usually borne by the client, Chile 15%, Peru 15% for technical assistance (30% for some other
services), El Salvador 20%. VAT on imported services is usually self-assessed by the client (reverse charge).

**B. Local partner (reseller invoices in local currency).** VAT is added to the invoice: IVA Mexico 16%, IVA
Colombia 19%, Brazil ISS + PIS/COFINS (no single VAT; ~14% planning), IVA Chile 19%, IGV Peru 18%, IVA El Salvador
13%. The partner issues the local e-invoice. Partner margin is negotiated separately and is not shown to the client.

Recommend scenario B when the client cannot gross up or needs a local e-invoice to deduct the expense; recommend A
when the treaty position is clean and the client accepts gross-up.

## 5. E-invoicing (local partner billing)
Mexico CFDI 4.0; Colombia factura electrónica validated by DIAN; Brazil NFS-e (municipal, with ISS); Chile DTE before
the SII; Peru comprobante electrónico before SUNAT; El Salvador DTE under Ministerio de Hacienda rules.

## 7. Payment terms and onboarding
- Preferred net 30. Country norms run longer (Colombia and Peru often net 45-60); anything above net 45 is an amber
  clause and needs Delivery Director approval.
- Expect supplier registration before the first invoice: RFC and CFDI data (MX), RUT (CO), CNPJ via the partner (BR).
  Add two weeks to the first invoice date.

## 8. Delivery model
LATAM SOWs default to nearshore delivery (clause CL-SUB-01) with the engagement director onshore when the account is
strategic. Use the nearshore rate card column; the gross-margin target (40%) and floor (32%) are the same as for US
SOWs.
