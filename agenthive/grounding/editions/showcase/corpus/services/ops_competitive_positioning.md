# Competitive Positioning Guide

**Owner:** Product Marketing · **Last reviewed:** 2026-10-26 · **Status:** current
Synthetic demo document — every competitor named here is invented for the demo.

## 1. How to use this guide
Argus scans the competitive watchlist every week and posts a digest to the sales space. Use this guide to decide what a
change means and how to respond in live deals. Three rules apply everywhere:
1. **Never disparage** a competitor in writing or in front of a client. Compare on outcomes and evidence.
2. **Never cite unverified sources** (anonymous forum threads, hearsay) to a client. Use them only as a prompt to ask a
   good discovery question.
3. **Never name competitors in public posts** (see the Social Media Policy).

## 3. Competitor profiles (fictional)
### Competitor A — Cobalt Cloud Partners
Workspace migration specialist with an offshore migration factory and aggressive per-user pricing.
- *Strengths:* price, speed to mobilise, volume.
- *Weaknesses:* thin change management; little AI or agent delivery experience; reports of slow post-cutover support.
- *How we win:* sell the outcome after cutover — adoption, floor-walkers, Gemini enablement. Quote the cost of a failed
  adoption (support tickets, shadow IT) rather than matching the migration price.
- *Price-cut response:* do **not** go below our pricing floor. Offer a phased approach or fixed-fee adoption bundle.

### Competitor B — Stratonix Consulting
Large multi-cloud systems integrator with an "agent factory" offering and packaged pilots.
- *Strengths:* brand with large enterprises, bench size, packaged 6-week pilots.
- *Weaknesses:* generalists across clouds; high day rates; long mobilisation.
- *How we win:* Google-specific specialisations, a working demo in the first meeting, a shorter Google-native pilot
  that reuses production patterns, and guardrails from day one.

### Competitor C — Quillon Digital
Boutique GenAI agency known for polished prototypes.
- *Strengths:* speed and design quality of prototypes.
- *Weaknesses:* limited security/governance practice; few production deployments.
- *How we win:* the prototype is step one — we bring the path to production (Morpheus demo → Thoth-reviewed SOW →
  guarded deployment) and an operating model.

### Competitor D — Halcyon Data Works
Data platform specialist with fixed-price warehouse migrations.
- *Strengths:* BigQuery and Looker depth; predictable pricing.
- *Weaknesses:* no collaboration practice; limited GenAI assistants.
- *How we win:* integrated bids (data platform + AI assistant + collaboration) and multi-system sync for real-estate
  and construction clients.

## 4. Reading watchlist changes
| Change type | What to do |
|---|---|
| Pricing | Tell active deal owners within 2 business days; prepare value framing, not a counter-price. |
| New offering | Compare scope, duration and price with our closest pattern; update the battlecard. |
| Hiring | Signals a market entry in 1-2 quarters; prepare references in that area. |
| Partnership | Check overlap with our pipeline; consider a joint-value story with our own partners. |
| Messaging | Share relevant thought leadership (blogs) with affected stakeholders. |
| Reputation (unverified) | Do not cite; use it to shape discovery questions. |

Impact levels: **high** = affects pricing or scope in active deals this quarter; **medium** = likely to matter within
two quarters; **low** = monitor.

## 5. Digest format (weekly, sales space)
1. Top 3 changes with impact and the recommended response.
2. Deals affected (by stage) — the AE confirms.
3. Market context from public news (cite sources; keep it separate from the synthetic watchlist).
4. Battlecard updates and proof points to use this week.
Keep it under 400 words; link to the watchlist for detail.
